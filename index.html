Engineering spotlights

🛡
Functional Safety
Turning a fault into a safe state before it could reach a clinical reading
The problem

Field feedback surfaced a quiet but serious risk: after certain fault conditions, the device could keep operating and display readings a clinician might trust, with no clear signal that the measurement could no longer be relied on. A device running in an unknown state is a patient-safety hazard, not just a defect.

Engineering approach

Designed a fail-safe Sleep Mode driven from Voice-of-Customer input through verification. On detecting a predefined fault, the device stops acquiring or displaying measurements, raises a clear alert, and requires a reset plus passing self-tests before returning to clinical use. New requirements were threaded through hardware power-state transitions, firmware fault-detection and recovery logic, and the system risk file at once, with traceability held from customer need to test.

Outcome

The failure path was closed by design — a faulted device can no longer present a reading as valid. Fault detection, alerts, and mandatory self-test-before-return were verified through functional, fault-injection, and regression testing, feeding directly into the 510(k) documentation package.

🧐
System Integration
Restructuring integration to deliver V&V data for an FDA submission
The problem

A closed-loop surgical instrument simulator was stalling because integration waited on full subsystem completion. Hardware, firmware, and software matured at different rates, so integration was always last and always late — and the simulator's output was the V&V data earmarked for FDA submission.

Engineering approach

Re-architected the integration strategy to give integration independent entry points at each development phase, enabling incremental validation against partial builds. Design outputs, verification evidence, and engineering reports were released in phase with milestones rather than assembled after the fact.

Outcome

Integration stopped being a source of schedule risk. Simulator validation completed on cycle and produced the V&V data package supporting the FDA submission, and the incremental-integration model became the team's standard for subsequent programs.

🏛
Traceability
Rebuilding a submission's traceability to clear an FDA inquiry
The problem

An FDA review returned observations against the software interface that displayed and managed a regulated device's data. The gaps were in the evidence, not the behavior: incomplete requirements traceability, verification records that no longer fully supported performance claims, and design documentation scattered across teams and years.

Engineering approach

Triaged each observation into documentation, verification, traceability, and performance-evidence categories. Reconstructed the Requirements Traceability Matrix end to end — user needs to requirements to design outputs to verification and validation — generating new records where history was missing, and coordinated targeted retesting where prior evidence fell short.

Outcome

The submission went back with a complete, defensible traceability chain and verification evidence that stood on its own, with improved DHF quality and a technically robust package for Regulatory Affairs.

🛠
Supplier Qualification
Requalifying a critical component to take $45 out of every instrument built
The problem

A precision instrument carried a single-source, high-cost needle assembly that set both its material cost and its supply risk. There was no qualified alternative and no measurement baseline showing where assembly variation came from, with a production ramp approaching.

Engineering approach

A Lean Six Sigma DMAIC project mapped the assembly workflow, and a Gauge R&R study established a trustworthy measurement baseline before any change was proposed. An alternate supplier was evaluated against the full performance and quality profile, then qualified through bench testing and process validation (IQ, OQ, PQ).

Outcome

The qualified alternate met every performance and quality requirement while removing roughly $45 of material cost per instrument — about $500K in annual savings at production volume — standardizing the assembly process and reducing single-source supply risk.

🔨
Reliability Engineering
Driving a home-use device through IEC 60601-1-11 reliability qualification
The problem

A medical electrical device intended for home healthcare had to prove it could survive real-world handling and years of use before production release. Early qualification runs exposed design weaknesses, and an epoxy-related manufacturing issue was quietly undermining durability.

Engineering approach

Built the reliability validation plan with Quality, R&D, and Mechanical Engineering — test procedures and acceptance criteria for ESD immunity, drop, mechanical shock, vibration, and accelerated life testing. Ran root cause analysis on every failed unit, traced failure mechanisms to design, materials, electrical assemblies, or process, and drove corrective actions through design review. Each change was re-run through the complete test sequence, not just the test it failed.

Outcome

The epoxy issue was resolved at its source, structural robustness improved, and qualification pass rates rose to roughly 90–95 percent. Accelerated life testing validated long-term durability, and the evidence package supported IEC 60601-1-11 compliance and production readiness.

🖥
Simulation & Test
Building a device emulator that freed verification from physical hardware
The problem

Software development, algorithm validation, and system testing were all gated on scarce physical hardware. Every test scenario depended on a real device being available, configured, and in the right state — slowing verification and making edge cases hard to reproduce.

Engineering approach

Analyzed the production device architecture — the ASIC and its communication interfaces, embedded algorithms, and hardware-software protocols — and mapped each behavior to a software simulation model. Wrote the emulator's system requirements and acceptance criteria, built the proof-of-concept that simulated sensor data and algorithm outputs across configurable device states, then verified it against production hardware through protocol checks and algorithm output comparison.

Outcome

The emulator produced repeatable, configurable test scenarios without hardware, accelerating verification by about 30 percent. Released under configuration control with full traceability, it became the engineering test platform for multiple Class II and Class III device programs.

💧
Sustaining Engineering
Upgrading a released device to IPX6 without disturbing its safety case
The problem

Usability feedback called for an existing device to withstand powerful water jets (IPX6). Sealing a released product touches nearly every subsystem — enclosure, connectors, PCB, firmware — and every change risked affecting electrical safety, performance, or the device's regulatory standing.

Engineering approach

Treated it as a system-level design change: design inputs and an ECR up front, then DFMEA and hazard analysis asking what happens if water gets in — moisture on the PCB, condensation-driven leakage current, patient exposure. Coordinated sealing, connector, and layout changes with mechanical and electrical engineering, and fault-monitoring and safe-state behavior with firmware, holding traceability from each new input to its verification.

Outcome

The change was carried through board- and device-level verification, ingress and environmental testing, and IEC 60601-1 / 60601-1-11 compliance checks, with updated risk file, DHF, and documentation supporting 510(k) and EU technical file updates and transfer back into production.

🖥
Platform Validation
Migrating validated IVD workstations to a new OS with proven equivalence
The problem

Instrument workstations running released IVD, research, and V&V software had to move from Windows XP to Windows 7. In a regulated lab, a new operating system is not an IT update — any change in instrument communication or data output could invalidate the validated baseline.

Engineering approach

Assessed and upgraded workstation hardware, then worked down the stack: FPGA code updates to keep instrument communication reliable, coordinated firmware changes, and updated LIMS interfaces and data export formats. Ran side-by-side workflow comparison studies between legacy and upgraded systems, plus regression and functional testing across IVD, research, and validation applications.

Outcome

Output equivalence between legacy and upgraded systems was demonstrated and discrepancies resolved before rollout. IQ/OQ and validation records were completed under quality procedures, and the upgraded workstations were deployed without disrupting instrument operation.

Manufacturing Transfer
Standing up and validating a manufacturing line for an NGS product
The problem

A Next-Generation Sequencing product was moving from development into production, and needed a manufacturing line that could build it repeatably, test it reliably, and satisfy quality system requirements from day one.

Engineering approach

Helped define the end-to-end manufacturing workflow with R&D, Quality, and Process Engineering. Designed custom test fixtures and production hardware, developed functional and range test methods covering nominal and worst-case conditions, ran Gauge R&R studies on the measurement systems, and executed IQ, OQ, and PQ on the equipment, applying statistical analysis to process capability.

Outcome

The line was qualified with validated equipment, trustworthy measurement systems, and automated test where it improved consistency — with validation protocols, reports, and DMR documentation in place under ISO 13485 and GMP.

Design control artifacts

📄
Design Input
System Requirements Document
Defines the system-level functional and performance requirements the device must meet. Captures stakeholder needs, intended use, user needs, and design inputs per FDA design control and ISO 13485 requirements.
The foundation of the design control process. Every downstream design output, test, and verification activity must trace back to this document.
SRD
💻
Design Input
Software Requirements Document
Specifies software functional and non-functional requirements per IEC 62304. Defines software items, interfaces, safety classifications, and performance criteria. Serves as the binding input for software architecture design and unit-level testing.
Drives the IEC 62304 software development lifecycle from architecture through unit and integration testing. Auditors expect this to be traceable in both directions.
SRD-SW
📋
Traceability
Requirements Traceability Matrix
Bidirectional traceability linking user needs through system requirements, software requirements, design outputs, verification tests, and validation evidence. The single artifact that demonstrates completeness of the design control chain to FDA and notified bodies.
Critical for 510(k) and PMA submissions. Auditors inspect this first to confirm every requirement is tested and every test is justified by a requirement.
RTM
⚠️
Risk Management
Risk Management File
End-to-end risk management documentation per ISO 14971 — covering hazard identification, probability and severity scoring, risk controls, residual risk evaluation, and benefit-risk analysis. Maintained as a living file throughout the product lifecycle.
Required for all medical device submissions. Demonstrates systematic harm prevention and that residual risk has been evaluated and accepted appropriately.
RMF
🔨
Architecture
Software Architecture Document
Describes software architecture, system interfaces, data flows, module decomposition, and design decisions for regulated software per IEC 62304 Section 5.3. Maps software items to safety classifications and defines the integration strategy across subsystems.
The technical backbone of the software design file. Reviewed in software audits by FDA and notified bodies to confirm architectural rigor.
SAD
✅
Verification
Verification and Validation Plan
Defines the V&V strategy, test methods, acceptance criteria, responsible parties, and schedule for confirming design outputs meet design inputs — verification — and that the finished device meets user needs — validation.
Keeps verification and validation clearly separated. Conflating the two is one of the most common audit findings in software-intensive device programs.
V&V Plan
🐛
Defect Management
Anomaly and Defect Tracking Log
Structured log of anomalies and defects identified during verification and validation, including severity classification, root cause, resolution, and regression test status. Required by IEC 62304 Section 5.6 for software problem resolution.
Demonstrates controlled handling of defects throughout development. Particularly critical for Class B and Class C software items under IEC 62304.
Anomaly Log
🛡️
CAPA
CAPA Report
Corrective and Preventive Action documentation covering problem identification, root cause analysis using fishbone, 5-Why, or fault tree methods, action plan, effectiveness verification, and formal closure per ISO 13485 Section 8.5.
One of the most scrutinized records in any FDA inspection. A well-structured CAPA with a demonstrated effectiveness check signals a functioning quality system.
CAPA
🌟
Phase Gate
Phase Gate Exit Review
Formal design review deck presented at each phase gate — Concept, Feasibility, Development, Verification, Validation, and Transfer. Documents exit criteria status, open actions, risk posture, and the go or no-go recommendation.
No phase advances without resolved open items and documented approval from the review board.
Gate Review
📁
Design History
Design History File Index
Master index of the Design History File cataloguing all design control records — user needs, design inputs, design outputs, design reviews, verification, validation, and design transfer documents.
An FDA investigator reviews this first. A clean, complete DHF index signals a controlled design process before a single underlying document is opened.
DHF Index
🔄
Design Transfer
Design Transfer Plan
Documents the transition of the design from R&D to manufacturing — covering production procedures, Device Master Record readiness, manufacturing process validation, training, and transfer acceptance criteria.
Often the most underdocumented phase of a device program. A robust transfer plan prevents the production nonconformances that trace back to incomplete handoffs from engineering.
